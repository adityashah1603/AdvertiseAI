# Patagonia (test brand) - Design Brain

Source of truth for brand tokens. Where any manifest, cache or prior asset
disagrees with this document, this document wins.

This is a research-generated test brand, not a customer submission. The
palette, voice and shape rules below are grounded in Patagonia's public brand
history and documented color/typography references (see Provenance). The
logo, mark, and type family are original placeholders, not reproductions of
Patagonia's real trademarks - see Provenance for why.

Last reviewed: 2026-08-11

## Palette

- primary: #223829 (pine - deep forest green)
- accent: #FB6526 (sunset orange)
- surface: #F4F1EA (stone - warm canvas, not white)
- ink: #000000
- muted: #6B6558

## Type

- heading: Public Sans
- body: Public Sans

- h1: 40px
- h2: 28px
- h3: 20px
- body: 16px

## Shape

- border-radius: 0px (square corners everywhere - cards, images, buttons, tags)

## Applying it

Patagonia is outdoor apparel and gear, built around one line the company
states as its purpose: "We're in business to save our home planet." Tone is
plain, declarative and present-tense. No exclamation marks, no
lifestyle-aspirational copy, no gradients, no soft rounded UI chrome. Say the
thing once, then stop.

Photography is real outdoor and product photography - unretouched, natural
light, worn gear, real weather. Never a studio plate, never stock-office,
never a lifestyle set that reads as a photoshoot rather than a place.

Surface is the stone tone, not pure white - it should read like uncoated
catalog paper, not a screen default. Ink is true black; do not lighten it for
"softness."

The wordmark is reserved for the logo lockup only. Never reset the brand name
in running headline or body copy as if it were the wordmark - use the body
face at whatever weight the hierarchy calls for. Never place the mark or
wordmark on a busy part of a photograph; give it a quiet, even-toned area or
a solid stone/pine ground.

Accent orange is reserved for a single call to action per canvas and for
nothing else - never a background fill, never a decorative shape, never more
than one per canvas. A primary CTA is solid pine fill with stone label text.
A secondary CTA is a 1px black outline on stone with black label text. Orange
is used only when the CTA is itself an urgent or activist call (e.g. a
petition or time-boxed action), and even then only as the label color inside
a black-outlined button - never as a fill.

Corners are square everywhere without exception - this is a harder rule here
than in most brands, because rounding is one of the fastest ways an outdoor
utility brand starts reading like a consumer lifestyle app.

## Provenance

Palette: primary and surface are inferred from Patagonia's real product and
catalog color language (earthy greens, uncoated stone/paper tones); accent
orange and ink black are grounded in documented sources - #000000 is
Patagonia's one broadly-documented official brand color, and the sunset
orange is drawn from publicly documented hex values associated with the
brand's mountain-skyline logo gradient. None of this is copied from an
official Patagonia brand guidelines PDF - one was not accessible - so treat
the exact hex values as informed, not authoritative.

Type: Patagonia's real wordmark is set in a modified Belwe Bold (a
commercial, licensed typeface not available to bundle here), and its digital
body copy is reported to use a proprietary in-house grotesque. Public Sans
(SIL OFL, US Web Design System) is substituted for both roles as an openly
licensed stand-in with a comparably plain, utilitarian grotesque character -
it is not a claim that Patagonia uses Public Sans.

Logo: the real Patagonia wordmark and mountain-skyline mark are registered
trademarks and are not reproduced here, reused from elsewhere, or
approximated closely enough to be mistaken for the original - the same
omit-and-flag treatment already used for the Duolingo generalization-test
brand in this project. `brand/patagonia-mark.svg`,
`brand/patagonia-logo.svg` and `brand/patagonia-logo-white.svg` are original
geometric placeholders (a peak silhouette, no borrowed artwork) built from
this document's own palette, for pipeline-testing purposes only.
