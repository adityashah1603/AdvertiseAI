# Oatly (test brand) - Design Brain

Source of truth for brand tokens. Where any manifest, cache or prior asset
disagrees with this document, this document wins.

This is a research-generated test brand, not a customer submission. The
palette, voice and shape rules below are grounded in Oatly's public brand
history and documented design-press coverage of its 2015 Forsman & Bodenfors
identity (see Provenance). The logo, mark, and type family are original
placeholders, not reproductions of Oatly's real trademarks - see Provenance
for why.

Last reviewed: 2026-08-11

## Palette

- primary: #ABCEE2 (oat blue - the pale sky blue most consistently cited as
  Oatly's signature color, carried by its flagship Original carton)
- accent: #E8462F (stamp red - a single, sparing "rubber-stamped" callout
  color)
- surface: #F6F1E4 (oat paper - a warm, uncoated cream, not white)
- ink: #131313
- muted: #8A9AA5

## Type

- heading: Space Grotesk
- body: Space Mono

## Type scale

- h1: 46px
- h2: 30px
- h3: 21px
- body: 16px
- caption: 12px

(Not sourced from an official numeric spec - no public type-scale document
was found during research. Authored to be consistent with Oatly's bold,
hand-set headline style as described in secondary sources. Treat as
provisional, same caveat as the type scale in the Duolingo generalization-test
brand in this project.)

## Shape

- border-radius: 6px on cards, images and containers (a soft, papery round -
  like a folded carton edge, not app-polished chrome); buttons and tags are
  fully pill-shaped (999px), read as a hand-stamped badge rather than a
  software control

## Applying it

Oatly is oat-based plant milk, built by Forsman & Bodenfors around a single
idea: treat every surface as editorial space. Tone is irreverent, plainspoken
and a little confrontational about dairy and sustainability ("Wow No Cow!",
"It's like milk, but made for humans," "the post-milk generation") - never
corporate, never a straight product-benefits list. Copy reads like it was
handwritten by one opinionated person, not a brand-voice committee. Headlines
can run long and conversational; it is fine for a headline to be a full
sentence with a joke or a jab in it.

Photography and illustration lean unpolished on purpose - flat color fields,
hand-drawn scribbles, doodled arrows and underlines used as real layout
elements, not decoration layered on top. Avoid glossy studio-dairy lighting,
avoid premium/aspirational food photography, avoid stock lifestyle scenes.
When a product or a real photograph appears, let it sit inside a flat color
field rather than a naturalistic scene.

Surface is the oat-paper cream, not pure white - it should read like an
uncoated printed carton, not a screen default. Ink is near-black and carries
almost all body copy; do not lighten it for "softness," the brand's whole
point is looking handmade and a bit rough at the edges.

The wordmark is reserved for the logo lockup only. Never reset the brand name
in running headline or body copy as if it were the wordmark - use the heading
face at whatever weight the hierarchy calls for. Keep the mark and wordmark
on a quiet, flat-color ground; never drop it onto a busy scribble field or a
photograph.

Accent red is reserved for a single stamped callout per canvas - a CTA badge,
a "new" flag, an underline on the one word that matters - and for nothing
else: never a background fill, never a decorative shape, never more than one
per canvas. A primary CTA is a solid ink-black pill with oat-paper label text.
A secondary CTA is a 2px black outline pill on oat-paper with black label
text. Stamp red appears only as the fill of a small circular or torn-edge
badge shape carrying the CTA label, angled slightly off-grid like an actual
rubber stamp - never as a button fill itself.

Corners are soft everywhere except the accent stamp, which is intentionally
irregular (a torn or circular badge, not a rounded rectangle) - the one place
this brand breaks its own grid on purpose.

## Provenance

Palette: primary oat blue is drawn from third-party brand-color trackers
(colorswall.com and comparable aggregators) that cite RGB(171,206,226) as
Oatly's primary/most recognizable brand color, consistent with the pale blue
that has fronted its flagship Original carton since the 2015 redesign. Ink
black is broadly corroborated - every source describing the identity calls
out black, hand-drawn lettering as the constant across all packaging. Surface
cream and accent red are inferred judgment calls, not tied to one confirmed
hex from an official guidelines document - one was not accessible - so treat
those two values as informed, not authoritative.

Type: Oatly's real headline typefaces are custom and proprietary - John
Rounded (named for creative director John Schoolcraft) and Toni Grotesk
(named for founder Toni Petersson) - and its body text is set in FF Magda
Clean, Cornel Windlin's distressed take on a Cubic typewriter design. None of
the three are freely licensed or bundleable here. Space Grotesk and Space
Mono (both SIL OFL, released as a companion family by Colophon Foundry and
distributed via Google Fonts) are substituted as open stand-ins - a friendly
grotesque paired with a typewriter-style monospace - chosen for a comparable
personality, not as a claim that Oatly uses these faces.

Logo: Oatly's real wordmark integrates a plant motif into the "A" of a
two-line lockup and is a registered trademark; it is not reproduced here,
reused from elsewhere, or approximated closely enough to be mistaken for the
original - the same omit-and-flag treatment already used for the Duolingo
generalization-test brand in this project. `brand/oatly-mark.svg`,
`brand/oatly-logo.svg` and `brand/oatly-logo-white.svg` are original
geometric/typographic placeholders (a simple oat-drop silhouette, no borrowed
artwork or plant-in-letterform trick) built from this document's own
palette, for pipeline-testing purposes only.
